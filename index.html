A LITTLE ABOUT ME

Why I make games

Growing up with games like Stardew Valley, Terraria and Elden Ring, I have come to love the RPG genre.

That is why I sincerely wish to one day help solve one of the genre's biggest problems: exploration becoming less and less fun towards the end, or just turning into a boss rush.

Besides that, since I started, I have always wanted to learn everything when it comes to game development, every step on the path of creating something new and amazing. After a while working on games and keeping track of the game industry just a bit more, I have found the certain steps that I really love working on.

First is building systems that work well together with my teammates. Creating that one script that feels like it can be expanded endlessly without much refactoring is just pure dopamine. Secondly, I like combining shaders, animation and particles for satisfying effects. It gives someone like me, without an artistic background, a chance to still make something beautiful.

PROJECT 01

Lord Red's Auction House

A visual novel set in a mysterious auction house, where you compete to win valuable artifacts and get closer to meeting Lord Red. Between auctions, you interact with four characters through branching dialogue choices that influence their relationships with you and determine which endings you can unlock.

My contributions were the auction system and visual effects. I created a turn-based auction system, where the different characters bid against you, using a weighted system to determine how much they would bid for each item. I also worked on a screen shake that increases in intensity as you continue bidding, helping communicate the growing risk of committing to an artifact. I made a shiny gloss shader for the coins to make them stand out as something you can interact with, and also a smoke shader to set the tone in different parts of the game.

This was my first major game project and my first time creating shaders. The smoke might have been the hardest one to get right, I could find references for creating fog, but smoke is less uniform, with irregular shapes, small gaps, and a warmer, yellowish-grey colour. Figuring out how to create that look was a challenge, but I really enjoyed experimenting with these effects and learning how they can transform a scene. Working on them made me realise how much I love creating VFX.

PROJECT 03

I Hate Spiders

I Hate Spiders is a tower-defense mobile game inspired by Bloons, where you place heroes to stop waves of spiders from reaching their destination.

I worked on the game's systems, including grid-based placement, spawning units during gameplay, and saving game progress. This was also my first game with a save system, teaching me the valuable lesson of how JSON files work.

The part I am happiest with is how I handled the heroes using ScriptableObjects. Most of each hero's data is stored in its own asset, so adding a new character mainly involves configuring its data rather than writing new code. This makes it super easy to add a new hero to the game, all you need is the art.

The project also taught me how important it is for the game's visuals and mechanics to be in sync. Combining my pixel art with an externally created map, whose spider paths didn't always align with the grid, made character placement feel a bit awkward. It's something I'd approach differently in a future project, particularly, being very careful when mixing art styles.

PROJECT 04

Castle Baan

A chess-inspired Sokoban puzzle game where you push pieces through levels. Each piece follows its own movement rules, and to complete a level, you must transform a Pawn into a Queen and reach the throne.

I worked on the core gameplay systems in C++ using SDL3, including chess-inspired movement, pushing mechanics, level loading, and undo/redo. I also implemented the Crown mechanic, which transforms a Pawn into a Queen. Beyond gameplay programming, I created pixel art for the game, including a layered parallax background for the main menu and a victory animation where the Queen piece transforms into a real queen.

One of the more challenging parts was getting the Pawn-to-Queen transformation and undo system to work together. Undo had to restore the correct game state, including the piece's identity and behaviour after it had transformed.

Building a game from scratch meant implementing many systems that are usually handled by a game engine. Although it sometimes felt like reinventing the wheel, it gave me a better understanding of how these systems work on a deeper level and how they interact. It also made me appreciate the freedom that comes with building your own tools: you can shape the underlying systems around the specific needs of your game, rather than being limited to what an engine provides out of the box.

PROJECT 05

Guard of the Crossing

This VR game is about slicing, blocking and dodging incoming objects that fly towards you. You stand in the middle of an intersection, so the objects can come from 3 different directions.

My contributions included setting up the VR controls and making the Unity VR template accessible for the rest of the team. My main focus was a custom sword skill system, featuring pose-based activation, movement tracking, progress-driven visual and audio effects, camera shake, controller haptics, and a projectile released upon a successful attack.

The idea behind the sword skill was to bring something I love about traditional action games into VR: the feeling of performing a powerful, cinematic attack. In conventional games, animations make these moments look impressive, but in VR, taking control away from the player's arms would feel unnatural. Instead, I wanted the player to be the animation.

The skill guides the player through the attack using visual, audio, and haptic feedback. The sword glows while the player performs the correct movements, with the glow disappearing if the skill fails. As the player raises the sword overhead, the camera shake intensifies until they reach the correct position. A controller vibration signals that the skill is ready, letting the player perform the final slash themselves.

PROJECT 06

Unreal Character Demo

So this is an interesting one, it is not a game, it is barely a demo. I am a programmer, and i wanted to make a bossfight, with cool animations. For some reason i thought it would be a good idea to make my own character, with my own animations. With no previous experience in Blender or 3D modelling, I spent the project learning the character creation pipeline, from modelling and sculpting to rigging, weight painting, and animation.

I eventually brought the character into Unreal Engine with my custom animations working. I didn't have time to finish texturing, so I experimented with materials, ambient occlusion, and vertex painting to give the character some visual definition.

Although the project didn't reach the boss fight I originally envisioned, it gave me a much better understanding of what's involved in creating a game-ready character. I learned how modelling, vertex groups, rigging, and weight painting fit together, and what artists need to consider to make a character work in-engine. As a gameplay programmer, I think understanding these processes helps me collaborate with artists just a bit better.
